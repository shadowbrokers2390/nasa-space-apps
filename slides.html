Ground Movement Near Infrastructure

Where is the ground moving under the things we built, and by how much? NASA-ISRO NISAR radar can show us.

The problem

Ground that moves a few centimeters a month breaks things slowly

  • Roads, pipes, foundations, and rail lines all assume the ground stays put.
  • Slow movement is invisible until pavement cracks or a pipe snaps.
  • Engineers cannot put sensors on every hillside and every city block.

They need to know where to look first.

Honest by design

A map people act on has to state its limits

  • The NISAR data is Provisional, and the app says so on every view.
  • Radar measures motion only toward or away from the satellite, so every number is "at least".
  • "On the moving area" does not mean "damaged", and the data does not prove a cause.
  • A third site, California's Central Valley, showed no consistent signal. It was left out, not dressed up.

The satellite shows where to look. Ground sensors confirm it: GNSS and survey points, borehole inclinometers, crack gauges, piezometers, and tiltmeters. The app lists what an engineer would check at each place.

How it was built

Open NASA and partner data made this possible

  • NASA-ISRO NISAR L-band radar, L2 GUNW interferograms, 103 date pairs, from the Alaska Satellite Facility through NASA Earthdata. ISRO is a Space Apps Space Agency Partner.
  • Processing: Python in Google Colab, reading only each site's pixels over the internet.
  • Map: a static web page with Leaflet, OpenStreetMap infrastructure, and an Esri base map.
  • AI use: the code and draft text were written with Claude (Anthropic), an AI assistant, and are labeled in every file. The measurements come from NISAR, not from an AI model.
